# Warm Storytelling Voice — Full 20-Min Rewrite (Grandfather by Firelight)
Narrator: wise, warm African grandfather, speaking TO one person, pauses, soft breaths, lived-through pain with peace.

[OPENING — 00:00-02:00]
There was a night... I remember a night — and perhaps you have had one too — where everything was quiet on the outside... but something inside was screaming. Not loudly. No. More like a low, dull ache. The kind that sits behind your eyes when you are lying in the dark and sleep will not come... and the thoughts just... keep coming.

[pause 3s]

You know that feeling? Where you love God — you really do — but somehow, somehow... your soul feels like it is at the bottom of a very deep well. And the worst part... is that you feel you should not feel this way. You tell yourself: I am a believer. I should have more faith than this.

[soft breath]

Beloved... can I tell you something today? The man who wrote Psalm 42... was a worship leader. He led others into the presence of God. And he wrote these words:

[SCRIPTURE CARD handwriting slowly appears]
Why, my soul... are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me?

He was not writing a theology textbook. He was crying out loud. On a page. To God. And God kept that cry in His Word... so that you would know today... that the valley you are walking through has been walked before. Come. Let us walk through it together.

[SECTION 1 — 02:00-04:30 Talk To Your Soul]
There is something the psalmist did that most of us never think to do. He stopped... and he talked to himself. Not the way we do when we are worrying — running the same anxious thoughts in circles. No. He stood up on the inside... and he spoke DOWN to his own soul. He said: Why are you so downcast?

Do you hear that? He interrogated his own despair. He did not just feel it and drown in it. He asked it a question.

[pause]

You see — most of our heaviness... stays heavy... because we just listen to it. We wake up at three in the morning and our feelings start preaching. They say: nothing will change. They say: you are alone. They say: God has forgotten your name. And we just... sit there. And nod.

[soft, almost amused]

But David — this broken, beautiful, complicated man — he looked at his own soul one day and he said: No. No, no, no. Put your hope in God. I will YET praise Him. Not I feel like praising Him. Not I will praise Him when things get better.

YET. Right now. In the middle of this. Before anything changes.

[pause]

So here is a gentle question I want to leave with you — just sit with it, do not rush past it — What has been preaching to your soul this week? Has it been your fears? Or has it been your Father?
