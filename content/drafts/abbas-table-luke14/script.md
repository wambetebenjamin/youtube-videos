# There Was Room For You

**Channel:** Abba's Table — Bible and communion · **Series:** At His Table (episode 1)
**Format:** long-form · **Engine:** `cinematic-film` · **Look:** `paper` (light desk, gentle stories)
**Running time:** ~4 min · **Tone:** quiet, unhurried, second person

> Channel fit: the channel promise is "a quiet place where Bible stories come alive and prayers
> draw you closer to God" — peace, healing, encouragement, for people seeking direction or comfort.
> So this isn't an argument. It's an invitation. The teaching is held inside a story, and the CTA
> is a prayer, not a prompt to comment a number.

---

## Opening — God's big door (sung, not preached)

> "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, into his courts with praise. Give thanks to him, and
> bless his name." — **Psalm 100:4 (WEB)**

You do not come crawling in.

That verse says **enter** — the way you enter a home you have been invited to. Gates, then courts,
then the door, and behind the door, a table that is already set.

But most of us have been standing on the step for years. Knocking politely. Offering to eat in the
kitchen. Apologising for our shoes.

And here is the thing He keeps saying, in story after story:

**There was room for you.**

Not room in general. Room *at the table*, with a place set, and a name written on it.

## The verse that makes people nervous

> "But when you are invited, go and sit in the lowest place, so that when he who invited you
> comes, he may tell you, 'Friend, move up higher.' Then you will be honored in the presence of
> all who sit at the table with you." — **Luke 14:10 (WEB)**

On the first read, this sounds like advice about being humble so you can be praised. But look at
what is really happening in that room.

**The host is moving people up.**

That's all. The host walks around the table and lifts people. He is not testing anyone to catch
them out — He is looking for someone to lift.

The whole scene starts because Jesus noticed something:

> "He spoke a parable to those who were invited, when he noticed how they chose the best seats."
> — **Luke 14:7 (WEB)**

We chose the best seats because we were afraid there wouldn't be one. That's the real fear. Not
pride. **Fear.**

And so we do the opposite in our thinking. We put ourselves last — not with the humility of a
guest who trusts the host, but with the fear of a child who has been turned away before.

There's a difference, and God knows it, and He comes for us either way.

## The end of the chapter — the door and the fire

Eight verses on, the same meal turns. A master sends his servant out to the streets:

> "Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in the poor, maimed, blind,
> and lame." — **Luke 14:21 (WEB)**

And when the room still isn't full:

> "Go out into the highways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled."
> — **Luke 14:23 (WEB)**

Look who has to be **compelled**.

It is not the honoured guest. It is the one standing in the hedge at the edge of the road, in the
dark, who has already decided they do not qualify and has stopped walking toward the door.

Maybe you have stopped walking toward the door.

I want to tell you what Scripture says about that, quietly, without any pressure:

> "Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you." — **James 4:8 (WEB)**

That is the whole transaction. You turn toward the house; the Father is already coming down the road.

## There is one thing this table asks

A table with a place for everyone is still a table **with a Host**. It is not a room without a
door; it is a home with an open door.

So there is one thing this table asks — and it is the same thing a family asks: come as family.

> "But as many as received him, to them he gave the right to become God's children, to those who
> believe in his name." — **John 1:12 (WEB)**

**Receive. Become.** That is the whole door. You do not clean up and then come. You come, and He
brings you in as a child of the house.

## Communion — take, and eat, and know you are welcome

At Abba's Table, we remember the table the way Jesus gave it to us:

> "Jesus took bread, and when he had blessed, he broke it, and gave to them, and said, 'Take, eat.
> This is my body.' He took the cup, and when he had given thanks, he gave to them. They all drank
> of it. He said to them, 'This is my blood of the new covenant, which is poured out for many.'"
> — **Mark 14:22–24 (WEB)**

There is no fence around this table except the door you are already standing at.

The bread is not a reward for a good week. The cup is not a prize.

It is a place set, for you, by the Host.

## Prayer

Father, thank You for the door that is open.

I have stood outside it so long that I started calling it closed. I have knocked politely and offered
to eat in the kitchen, and all the while the place was already set.

Tonight I would like to sit down.

Thank You that the Spirit who brought me to Your table is the same Spirit who calls You *Abba* —
Father. Thank You that I am not an honoured guest but a child of the house.

Seal this promise in me, according to Your word: **Whoever comes to You will never be cast out.**

And Father, for the one still standing in the hedge — the one who thinks the room is full —
go out and compel them in. Bring them home.

In Jesus' name, amen.

## Closing

> "He who comes to me I will in no way throw out." — **John 6:37 (WEB)**

There was room for you.

There still is.

**Sit, listen, and encounter God — one story, one prayer at a time.**
