# Peace, Be Still

**Channel:** Abba's Table — Bible stories · **Series:** Stories He Told and Lived (episode 1)
**Format:** long-form · **Engine:** `cinematic-film` · **Look:** `paper` (warm, calm)
**Running time:** ~7 min · **Shape:** pure story retelling, closing in prayer

> **Passage:** Mark 4:35–41, with Matthew 8:24–27 as a parallel. Quoted from the **World English
> Bible** (public domain) — see `verses.md`.
>
> **Storytelling rule for this channel:** no interpretation until the story has finished. We do not
> explain the storm while it is still blowing. We tell it as it happened, in the order it happened,
> and let the last question in the passage — *"Who then is this?"* — be the thing the listener is
> left holding. Only then do we pray.

---

## 1. Evening, and an ordinary sentence

It had been a long day by the water.

All afternoon Jesus had been teaching from a boat, because the crowd on the shore was so thick He
could not stand among them. He had told them about seed and soil, about lamps and measures, about a
mustard seed that starts smaller than everything and ends up taller than a man.

By evening He was tired.

> "On that day, when evening had come, he said to them, 'Let's go over to the other side.'"
> — **Mark 4:35 (WEB)**

That is all He said. Not a warning. Not a forecast. Just: let's go over to the other side.

And they went. They left the crowd standing on the shore, took Him into the boat exactly as He was —
still dusty, still probably with the day in His shoulders — and pushed off. Mark adds a small detail
that matters later: **other small boats were with them.** They were not alone out there. This was not
a private crossing. There were witnesses.

Several of these men had fished this water their whole lives. They knew the Sea of Galilee. They knew
how fast it could turn — cold air off the eastern hills dropping onto warm water without warning.
They were not afraid of boats.

## 2. The storm

> "A big wind storm arose, and the waves beat into the boat, so much that the boat was already
> filled." — **Mark 4:37 (WEB)**

Matthew says the boat was *covered* with the waves.

It came up fast, the way it does on that lake. The wind first — a sound like a wall. Then the water.
Not spray; weight. Waves breaking over the side and into the hull, and the men bailing with whatever
their hands found, and the bailing not keeping up.

These were fishermen. And they were losing.

And in the middle of it, Mark records something that has troubled readers for two thousand years:

> "He himself was in the stern, asleep on the cushion." — **Mark 4:38 (WEB)**

Asleep.

Not praying. Not bracing. Not calming the sea before it got bad. Asleep, on a cushion, in the back of
a boat that was filling with water.

## 3. The question they asked

> "They woke him up, and told him, 'Teacher, don't you care that we are dying?'"
> — **Mark 4:38 (WEB)**

*Don't you care.*

Not "save us." Not "help." The first thing out of their mouths is not a prayer, it is a wound.

Matthew has them say *"Save us, Lord! We are dying!"* — Mark keeps the accusatory edge. Both are true
at once. When you are terrified and someone who could help is asleep, the fear comes out as an
accusation. That is what fear does when it has been left alone too long.

And note what they did **not** do. They did not lose their faith. They went to Jesus. They went to
Him angry, and frightened, and half-accusing — but they went to Him, in a boat, in the dark, and they
woke Him.

## 4. Peace, be still

> "He awoke, and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, 'Peace! Be still!'
> The wind ceased, and there was a great calm." — **Mark 4:39 (WEB)**

Mark uses the same word for *rebuked* that he uses when Jesus silences a demon. He is not calming
weather. He is speaking to something.

**Peace. Be still.** In the Greek, it is closer to *be silent, be muzzled* — the same command He gives
to an unclean spirit. He stands up in a boat, in a storm that professional sailors have already lost
to, and He speaks four words.

And it stopped.

Not gradually. Not "the wind died down over the next hour." **There was a great calm.** Mark uses a
word that means a deep, flat, unnatural stillness — the kind that makes men more frightened than the
storm did.

> "He makes the storm a calm, so that its waves are still." — **Psalm 107:29 (WEB)**

The psalmist had written that about God. The men in the boat watched a man do it.

## 5. Why are you afraid

Now the sea is flat, and the storm is over, and you would expect relief. Instead:

> "He said to them, 'Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?'"
> — **Mark 4:40 (WEB)**

He does not say *why did you wake me.* He does not say *I told you we were going to the other side.*
He asks about the fear.

Because the fear was not really about the water. The water only made it visible.

They had been in a boat with Him the entire time. The storm did not change who was in the boat. It
only revealed what they actually believed about Him — that He might sleep through their dying, that
He might not care.

And here is the mercy in the question: He asks it **after** the calm. He does not interrogate them in
the wind. He gets them safe first, and then He asks them to look at their own hearts.

## 6. Who then is this

> "They were greatly afraid, and said to one another, 'Who then is this, that even the wind and the
> sea obey him?'" — **Mark 4:41 (WEB)**

They went out afraid of the storm. They came back afraid of Him.

That is the last verse of the story, and it is left open on purpose. Mark does not tell us the answer.
He tells us the question, and he leaves it standing at the edge of the water for every reader after
them.

*Who then is this.*

Maybe that is the question you are meant to carry out of this story tonight. Not a lesson about
storms. Not a promise that your storm will end on your schedule. Just this: **there is a man in your
boat who can say peace to the wind.**

## Prayer

Father, thank You that You were in the boat.

You were not on the shore watching us drown. You were in the stern, in the same water, in the same
night. Whatever we are facing tonight, You are not far off from it — You are in it with us.

Forgive us for the ways our fear has sounded like accusation. Forgive us for believing that Your
silence meant You did not care. You were not indifferent; You were at rest. Teach us the difference.

Lord Jesus, You spoke to the wind and it stopped. Speak to what is rising in us tonight. Where there
is noise, say peace. Where there is chaos, say be still. Where there is a storm we have been bailing
out for years, stand up in our boat.

And when the water is flat, give us the courage to answer Your question honestly — to look at our own
fear and call it what it is — and then to ask the question the disciples asked, and to keep asking it
all our lives:

**Who then is this?**

We want to know You, not just Your calm. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Closing

> "In peace I will both lay myself down and sleep, for you, Yahweh alone, make me live in safety."
> — **Psalm 4:8 (WEB)**

He slept in the storm because He trusted the Father in the storm.

Tonight, if you can, sleep. He is still in the boat.
