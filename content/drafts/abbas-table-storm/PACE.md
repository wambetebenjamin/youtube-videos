# Pace — measured, not guessed

The narrator was called "still very fast" after the 12:34 cut. That was correct, and the reason was
diagnosable: **the stretch was applied to the wrong place.**

## What the numbers say

| Cut | Length | Words | Overall wpm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First edition | 632 s | 1762 | **167** |
| Current cut | 754 s | 1762 | **140** |
| Reference (BibleCue 18:53, ~2260 words) | 1133 s | ~2260 | **~120** |

167 wpm is *minutes per page*, and it is the single reason the first edition felt hurried — not the
length. Stretching the whole track to 0.85 pulled the average down to 140, but it pulled everything
down equally: the pauses got longer too, so the read itself was still fast and the film merely
stopped more often. Listening to it, the ear hears the *speaking rate*, not the average.

## The reference, measured

The hook runs **0:00 to about 1:37** and is **403 words**, i.e. **~250 wpm** — brisk. That is
deliberate and it is the lesson: the reference is not slow throughout. It is **fast through the
setup, then slow and spacious through the body**, where each sign gets its own scene, its own
scripture, and a long explanatory paragraph. The overall 120 wpm is the average of a quick opening
and a very unhurried middle.

So the fix is not "slow everything by another 15%". It is:

1. **The voice itself must be slower.** No amount of post-stretch fixes a read that arrives hurried;
   stretching adds warp, not calm.
2. **The opening should stay comparatively quick** — measured and purposeful, like the reference's.
3. **The body should be much slower than the average** — that is where the length belongs.

## The target

- Overall **~115–120 wpm**, matching the reference.
- Speaking rate after stretch **~148 wpm** (the ~160 wpm voice, taken to ~0.92).
- Length **~15 minutes**, which is where 1762 words lands at 118 wpm.

## Consequences

- **A different voice.** Measured on a probe line: the chosen voice reads near 160 wpm. That is
  right, and it is still not slow enough alone.
- **The score must be rebuilt** — the audio was lost to a sandbox reset and the mix must be redone.
- **The music was inaudible** because the bed is voiced at D2–A3 (73–220 Hz) with almost nothing
  above 2 kHz; on phone and laptop speakers that is simply not there. The score is being re-voiced
  an octave up with real content in the 200 Hz–1.5 kHz band and lifted in the mix.
